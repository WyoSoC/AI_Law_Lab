AI Law Lab

A research platform for running reproducible experiments on how language models reason about law — contracts, case law, statutes and regulation — executed on the University of Wyoming's own DGX Spark cluster, so no document or query ever leaves campus.

Design an experiment Browse legal sources Open the dashboard

Why it exists

Legal AI claims are easy to make and hard to check. A demo that looks convincing once tells you very little about whether a model reasons soundly, cites real authority, or behaves consistently when the facts shift. AI Law Lab exists to turn those claims into experiments: every run snapshots its exact configuration and records a full trace, so a result is something you can re-run, compare, and cite — not a screenshot. Because inference runs on local hardware, the lab can be used with sensitive or unpublished material that a commercial API would be the wrong home for.

What it does

📄

Document analysis

Plan a reading of a contract or opinion, retrieve grounded passages with page anchors, and produce an answer whose every claim is traceable to a citation a human can open.

🛠️

Agentic workflows

A tool-using loop that can search the corpus, do damages arithmetic, and — when explicitly enabled — reach the live legal databases, with every tool call recorded for audit.

⚖️

Multi-agent role-play

Stand up opposing counsel as distinct agents, each with its own backstory, temperament and private memory, and watch a negotiation or hearing play out turn by turn, then get it assessed.

🗂️

Grounded retrieval

Upload PDFs or pull directly from CourtListener, the Federal Register, the eCFR, govinfo and SEC EDGAR — everything is chunked, embedded, and made citable by topic.

How a study runs

  1. Gather sources. Upload documents or search the online legal databases and ingest the results you want, grouped by topic.
  2. Design an experiment. Pick a mode and configure it through the builder — no JSON required — then save it.
  3. Launch a run. Provide the run's inputs; work is dispatched across the Spark cluster and streamed back live as a trace.
  4. Read the result. Every run keeps its config snapshot, citations, per-host timing and full event log, so it can be compared or reproduced later.

Runs execute real language-model calls against shared research hardware. Please be considerate of others using the cluster.
